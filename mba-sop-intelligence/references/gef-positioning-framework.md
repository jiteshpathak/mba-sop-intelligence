# GEF Positioning Framework (Non-Stereotyped)

## Principle

General Engineer Female candidates are not a template class. Positioning must be individualized and evidence-based.

## Typical Competition Reality

- Engineering-heavy applicant pools create narrative compression risk.
- Differentiation usually comes from depth, decision quality, and business framing, not demographic framing.

## Useful Positioning Angles (choose only if evidence exists)

1. **Problem-to-impact arc**
   - Technical execution -> business consequence -> leadership behavior.
2. **Decision ownership**
   - Moments where candidate made trade-offs under constraints.
3. **Cross-functional translation**
   - Evidence of bridging technical and non-technical stakeholders.
4. **Growth through ambiguity**
   - Transition from task ownership to outcome ownership.
5. **Distinctive non-work signal**
   - Sustained extracurricular/volunteering pattern showing character and initiative.

## Weak Positioning Patterns to Avoid

- "As a woman engineer, I automatically bring diversity."
- Resume-dump storytelling with no reflection.
- Inflated leadership labels without ownership details.
- Generic "I want to become a CEO" goals without intermediate plan.

## Interview Defensibility Test

Any sentence in SOP should pass:

- What exactly happened?
- What did you personally do?
- What changed because of you?
- What did you learn that led to MBA timing now?
